### Correction, 2026-08-20: the additional-location price had to become per-plan

This row used to be a single **$229**. That is fine against Standard's $249 base and **breaks
against Starter's $99**: two Starter accounts cost $198 where one two-location Starter account cost
$328, so a customer saved $130 by splitting. Same arbitrage as the $269 draft, smaller, and it
survived into the decided sheet because only the Standard line was ever checked against the
invariant. The row even carried two costs ($61.20 / $18.44) against one price, which was the tell.

Caught by the guard test, not by re-reading the sheet, which is the argument for having written the
test. The fix follows the sheet's own rule: "additional locations run on the same plan as the first"
has to mean **the same price**. Starter locations are priced at parity with the Starter base, and
Standard keeps its deliberate 8% discount ($229 against $249).

**Annual is two months free** (16.7%), which lands within a couple of dollars of a clean ×1.2 on
every line. Not "20% off", which would force ugly monthly numbers ($125, $311) for three extra
points.

Standard is labelled **Most popular**. Three columns, with Multi-Location as contact-us, so Standard
reads as the middle choice.

**No $499 tier.** It was 47% margin and it sold locations at 55% of the Standard price while each
one cost more to serve.

## 2. Why it is shaped this way

**Cadence is the cost. Competitors are nearly free.**

| Change | What it costs us |
| --- | --- |
| 3 → 10 competitors | +$16.88/mo |
| weekly → daily briefs | **+$37.93/mo** |

So: cadence is the tier, locations are the meter (priced flat), competitors are cheap and
generous. Price is **linear in the cost drivers**, which is what makes the grid arbitrage-free.

### The arbitrage this replaced

An earlier draft priced Starter at $99 for one location and an *additional* location at $269. Bryan
found the hole: two Starter accounts is $198 for two locations, against $568. The customer saved
$370 by opening a second account.

Root cause: the add-on was priced off a **daily** location's cost while Starter sells a **weekly**
one. **Non-linear price over a linear cost driver always creates arbitrage.**

### Arbitrage tests the final sheet passes

- **Add-on location ($229) is below the base ($249)**, so a second account is never cheaper. Hard
  rule: **the add-on can never exceed the base.**
- **Competitors at $10** beat stacking at every level: 6 competitors is $129 versus $198 for two
  accounts; 12 competitors is $189 versus $396 for four.
- **Additional locations run on the same plan as the first.** Stated on the page, no footnote. It
  matches how the system already works, since cadence comes off the org's tier.
- A customer who splits accounts to mix cadence gets **74.5%** margin from us versus 71.1% for
  all-Standard. Splitting cannot hurt us; it just means they bought less. Per-location cadence is
  therefore **upside to capture later, not a leak to plug.**

### Two findings worth remembering

**The Aug 13 model migration was a real 62% win, not a degradation.** Sonnet 4.6 + Opus 4.8 →

lesson as DataForSEO: a metric that is not reconciled against a bill is a guess.

## 4. Velocity, and why $99 is not a loss

At $99 the contribution margin is **77.5%** and **12 customers cover the entire fixed floor.** We
are never losing money on a customer at any price on this sheet.

The OpenAI comparison does not apply to us, and that is the good news: their losses are training, a
fixed cost in the billions. Ours is $875/month with a per-customer contribution of 77%. The
burn-and-raise playbook exists for businesses whose marginal cost exceeds price. That is not us.

What $99 costs is not profit, it is **how fast opex gets covered**:

| Monthly opex | Customers needed |
| --- | --- |
| $0 (floor only) | 12 |
| $5,000 | 77 |
| $10,000 | 142 |
| $20,000 | 272 |

And the scenario that worried us most does not break it: if beta forces daily briefs on Starter,
contribution falls to $38.84, and it is **still positive**, floor covered at 23 customers instead of 12.

**Annual prepay discipline.** $99 annual collects $1,188 on day one against $229 of annual COGS.
**$879 of that is unearned revenue, a liability.** Recognise 1/12 a month. Annual prepay funds the
floor and reserves, never fixed opex. That is the trap that kills companies while they show growth.

## 5. Multi-Location (contact us)

No printed price. Quote from a published internal schedule so nobody negotiates from scratch.

| Locations | Off | Price/location | Margin |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 to 5 | 0% | $229.00 | 70.4% |
| 6 to 19 | 10% | $206.10 | 67.4% |
| 20 to 49 | 12% | $201.52 | 66.7% |
| 50+ | 15% | $194.65 | 65.7% |

**Volume does not make us cheaper.** Per-location margin is identical at 1 location and at 200: cost
is linear and the floor is already covered. No vendor rate steps down until ~187 locations. So a
discount is never justified by size alone: it is justified by what we get back.

**Floor prices, contribution only** (a 50-location chain covers the fixed floor by itself):

| Cadence | Our cost | 70% floor | 65% | 60% | 50% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| daily, 5 competitors | $61.20 | **$226** | $191 | $165 | $130 |
| weekly, 3 competitors | $18.44 | **$68** | $57 | $50 | $39 |

List is $229 and the 70% line at daily is $226, so **there is almost no discount room on daily
cadence.** The lever is cadence, not price: 10 daily + 50 weekly at 12% off is $71k ARR at **71.2%**,
better margin than all-daily because they bought less.

### Rules for whoever takes the call

1. Quote **cadence mix first**, discount second.
2. **Up to 10%: approve freely** (67%+).
3. **10% to 15%: standard for volume**, per the schedule. Costs 3.0 to 4.7 margin points. On a
   60-location chain that is +$145k ARR for $19.8k/year forgone, roughly 48% revenue growth for
   four margin points. Not a close call.
4. **Tie the discount to the asset.** We discount for fundraising and M&A optionality, and that only
   exists if we can use it: **named reference rights, a publishable result, annual prepay,
   multi-year term.** If they will not give reference rights, price at 10%, not 15%, because the strategic
   asset is not being delivered.
5. **Past 15%:** must beat the realistic alternative in **absolute contribution dollars**, and only
   as a one-time or stepped concession. Never a permanent rate. Avoided CAC is one-time
   (~$30k on 60 locations); a 20% permanent discount is $33k **every year.**
6. **Hard floor $165/location on daily.** Below that is an explicit decision to buy a logo, with a
   named end date.
7. **Any deal over ~20 locations pilots one region first**, paid, 90 days.

### Two risks to hold in view

**Concentration cuts against the story we are buying.** 60 locations at $145k against ~$300k total
is 48% of revenue in one account, and diligence discounts concentrated ARR. Land the chain *and*
keep the long tail growing.

**Delivery is the binding constraint, not price.** No org in production has ever had **more than one
location**, and first run takes 21 minutes with a five-minute cron in the loop. Sixty simultaneous
onboardings is the risk.

## 6. Support: the cost, and how it was derived

**Booked figures: $10 per Starter location per month, $21 per Standard location per month.**
Add-ons: **$4** per additional Starter location, **$8** per additional Standard location, **$1.42**
per additional competitor.

These are **constructed bottom-up from failure points, not measured.** Production cannot yield a
rate yet: 8 orgs, 8 locations, 13 profiles, and the only inbound is 8 feedback rows from 2 prompted
beta testers with no money at stake. Treat this as a booking placeholder that replaces a $0 that was
never true, and expect to revise it with real data around November.

### The dominant failure mode is "is this right?", not "how do I?"

Of the 8 real feedback rows in production (2026-07-22 to 2026-08-17), **6 are data-correctness
complaints**, and two of those are specifically count mismatches: the system claiming to watch 5
competitors while naming 3, and a reputation card citing two reviews while showing one. A third is
ALT-636 surfacing exactly as predicted, reported as pills that are "not related to my area".

Two consequences shape the model:

1. **This class cannot be closed by automation or a doc link.** Someone has to open the account and
   look at the data. That is why the deflection assumption below is 50% and not 80%.
2. **After this pricing ships, a count mismatch stops being a bug report and becomes a billing
   dispute.** Competitors become a purchased quantity. "You said 5 and showed 3" is then a refund
   conversation, not a papercut.

A third consequence is structural: **support scales with brief volume, not only with account count.**
A daily location generates roughly 7x the claim surface of a weekly one. That is convenient, because
the load lands on the tier that charges more for it.

### The build-up

| Component | Starter (weekly) | Standard (daily) |
|---|---|---|
| Tickets per account per month | 1.0 | 2.5 |
| Automation tokens, $1.25/ticket | $1.25 | $3.13 |
| Human: 50% deflection, 20 min, $35/hr | $5.83 | $14.58 |
| Onboarding, amortised over 12 months | $3.00 | $3.00 |
| **Booked** | **$10.00** | **$21.00** |

Three judgement calls, stated so they can be argued with:

- **$35/hour loaded, not $0 and not founder cost.** Support is Bryan and Chris today, so the cash
  cost is tokens only. Booking $0 is what let a discount schedule get approved without being tested
  against it. Booking founder cost ($150+/hr) means nothing clears. $35 is a findable DFW contract
  rate for someone who can read a dashboard and escalate, with padding.
- **50% deflection.** Automation closes "how do I" and "change my setting". It does not close "is
  this data right", because closing that means either agreeing it is wrong (an engineering fix) or
  explaining why it is right (trust not yet earned in month one). This is the number to drive down.
- **Support is per-ACCOUNT, not per-location.** One owner calls once whether they run one store or
  four. Add-on locations therefore carry about 40% of a base line, not a full one.

**Automation does not make support cheap.** At these figures the human line is 88% of the cost. What
automation buys is a slower growth curve.

### ⚠️ Do not "correct" these numbers with early observed data

The 20 minutes above is a **steady-state** figure: the cost of running a known process. For the
first stretch, every ticket is a fresh investigation, because there are no scripts, no catalogued
failure modes, and no fast path for even the routine things. Measuring minutes-per-ticket in that
window measures the cost of **inventing** a process, not of running one, and it will read far
higher than the truth.

Booking that number would inflate the support line, and because it arrives as real observed data it
would look more trustworthy than the estimate it replaced. **A future reader with three months of
early tickets in hand should not raise these figures on that basis.**

The measurement becomes meaningful at the point there are known failure modes with fast paths: when
a password reset takes 90 seconds because someone wrote down how, the 90 seconds is a real number.
Until then this stays an estimate and is labelled as one. Bryan's call, 2026-08-20, and it is the
reason no support-effort instrumentation is being built into the support form (ALT-695).

### Where the risk actually concentrates

Inverted from intuition: **the single-location Starter account is the exposure, not the chain.**

- A 4-location Standard account is $936/month of revenue. $25/month of support is 2.7% of it.
- A single Starter account is $99/month. The same $25 takes 78% contribution down to about 53%.

So the segment being optimised for volume, to get subscriber count up for a raise, is the same
segment where support hurts most per dollar. Onboarding automation aimed at the single-location
Starter is the highest-leverage support investment, and it is also the most automatable case.

**The chain constraint is capacity, not margin.** A 20-location chain runs about **3.6 human hours
per month** ongoing (25 min base plus ~10 min per extra location). Two founders can carry roughly
five of those before support is somebody's actual job. Onboarding 60 locations is about **15.5
hours** once (45 min for the account plus ~15 min per location), which is real but not alarming.
Treat the ongoing hours as a hiring trigger.

### Support costs already sitting in the code

Found while deriving the above, all fixed or filed:

- **The competitor swap cooldown was 30 days** (`lib/billing/limits.ts`). Starter gets 3 competitors,
  auto-discovered and approved with a fast click during onboarding. Picking wrong meant a 30-day
  lockout with no self-serve fix, landing in week one when the relationship is newest. **Changed
  2026-08-20** to two swaps during the trial and then one per 7 days.
- **No ownership or role-change action exists.** Team actions are invite and remove only, so every
  ownership or manager change is a manual database touch. Filed; the product has 6 distinct owner
  users across 8 orgs today, so this is imminent rather than theoretical.
- **Three support addresses across two domains** were reachable from the product
  (`support@alivelabs.co` in the Stripe portal route, the held-account panel and the checkout error
  copy; `support@getticket.ai` in the error-report fallback). Consolidated 2026-08-20 to
  `support@getticket.ai` via `lib/support/contact.ts`. `info@getticket.ai` remains valid for general
  contact in the marketing footer only, and still needs to be created.

## 7. The two margin gates

A realistic support number **does not fit inside this price sheet at a 70% per-line floor.** With
support booked, every line falls short:

| Line | Excluding support | Including support |
|---|---|---|
| Starter $99 | 78% | 67.9% |
| Standard $249 | 72% | 63.6% |
| Additional location $229 | 70% | 66.5% |
| Competitor $10 | 76% | 61.7% |

A 3-location Standard account blends to 65.5%.

**The resolution is to define two gates rather than reprice.** Support in COGS is a reporting
choice, not a fact: plenty of comparable companies book it in G&A. So:

- **Gate 1, external and unchanged: 70% contribution excluding support.** This is what section 3
  measures and what gets quoted. Every list price clears it.
- **Gate 2, internal: 60% fully loaded, including the section 6 support figures.** Every list price
  clears this too. What it tests is discounts, which is exactly where the pressure belongs.

Gate 2 changes one price and one rule.

**Price: the competitor add-on moves from $10 to $15 annual / $18 monthly.** Confirmed by Bryan 2026-08-20, with the reasoning that a price *cut* later delights customers while a rise does the opposite, so the higher of two defensible numbers is the safer starting point. At $10 it lands at
61.7% and is the only list line that fails gate 2. $15 puts it at 74.5%. It is a small absolute
number that will not lose a deal, and it preserves the property that every list line clears both
gates, which is worth more than the $5.

**Rule: the Multi-Location discount schedule is now testable.**

| Deal shape | Fully loaded |
|---|---|
| Base location at 15% off | **57.1%. Fails gate 2.** |
| 20-location chain at 12% off, blended | 61.8%. Clears. |

This supports the 10% to 12% cap already decided, and shows where 15% is actually dangerous: **on a
small discounted deal, not a large one.** A big chain blends its discount across many add-on
locations, which carry the lighter support load. A small one does not. **Reserve 15% for deals large
enough to blend, and never grant it on a one- or two-location deal.**

The **$165/location hard floor** on daily from section 5 still holds and is unaffected: it was set
on variable cost and remains above it.

## 8. Still open, and they are judgement calls

**Two items closed on 2026-08-20.** *Target margin* is settled: 70% excluding support as the external
gate, 60% fully loaded as the internal one (section 7). Bryan's reasoning, recorded because it sets
the bar for everything else: the goal is subscriber count to prove the model and raise capital, not
near-term profit, and a profitability story that holds up in diligence is worth more than the margin
points. 70% also sits at the top of the AI-native band (50% to 70% is typical where per-unit
inference is real), which is a defensible number rather than a classic-SaaS 80% that cannot be
supported. *Support cost* is settled at section 6, replacing the $0.

Still genuinely open:

1. **Deflection rate, assumed at 50%.** The single biggest lever in section 6, and the one that is
   measurable from day one **if** every inbound is stamped with an account and minutes spent, even
   while founders are the ones working it. Sweat equity with a timer is data. Without one it stays
   the $0 that was there before. This is the instrumentation that makes the November revision
   evidence-based instead of another estimate.
2. **Tickets per account per month, assumed at 1.0 and 2.5.** Needs real paying customers. The
   current 8 rows come from prompted testers, which biases the rate in both directions at once.
4. **Multi-product allocation.** Supabase, Vercel and Firecrawl are shared accounts (Supabase has
   five projects, two of them neither Ticket nor Neat). Every fixed-cost figure here uses a
   Ticket-share convention that has not been ratified.
5. **PostHog volume** is the one estimated input (~1,000 events/customer/month).
6. **Top-of-range has zero observations.** Nothing above 5 competitors or 1 location has ever run.
   Treat multi-location cost as provisional until a real one exists.
